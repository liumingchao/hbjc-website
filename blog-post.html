What does it actually cost to ship an expandable house?

Almost every quotation you receive stops at FOB. That is the number that matters least. Here is a complete landed-cost breakdown for two 20ft units going to Sydney — every line, including the ones that show up on the invoice three weeks after you thought you were done.

Two 20ft expandable units and the fittings crate, one 40HQ container.

The number every buyer anchors on is the FOB price. Then reality arrives in a series of invoices: ocean freight, terminal handling at both ends, customs clearance, quarantine inspection, cartage, and the crane you forgot to book. This article takes one real order and follows the money all the way to the driveway.

The order we are pricing

Two 20FT expandable container houses, standard layout, 50 mm EPS panels, 220 V wiring, delivered to a residential property in western Sydney. Both units fit one 40HQ container, which is the entire reason this configuration is the cheapest per square metre to land.

The FOB price was $9,000. Preparing the site and getting the units from the wharf to the block cost $2,600 — 29% on top. Plan for that before you compare quotes on price alone.

Where quotes differ wildly, and why

  • Ocean freight timing. The same container that costs $2,400 in March can cost $4,100 in October. Ask for a rate valid for the month of sailing, not a rate "indicative" for the year.
  • Terminal charges at destination. These are not negotiable and not controlled by us. Any supplier who quotes you a total and omits them is either inexperienced or hoping you will not notice.
  • Container detention. Free time at the port is typically 3–7 days. If your site is not ready, this is $80–200 per day. Do not ship before the base exists.
  • Whether the units fit one container. Two 20ft units sharing a 40HQ is roughly 40% cheaper per unit in freight than shipping two 40ft units separately. Model choice is a freight decision as much as a floor-plan decision.

Three ways to reduce the landed cost

  1. Choose a folded size that shares a container. This is the single biggest lever and it is decided at the specification stage, not at the booking stage.
  2. Buy the base work locally. Blocks, hardcore and labour are all cheaper in your own market than imported. We supply the base drawing with point loads marked so your contractor can price it accurately.
  3. Take delivery with a sideloader if your site allows it. A sideloader drops the container without a crane in a lot of residential situations, and it usually costs less than a crane hire window.

What we send you with the quotation

Our quotations put FOB and CIF side by side, list the destination charges as estimates with the assumption stated, and flag the three items that are outside our control — freight rate movement, port detention and local cartage. If a competing quote is materially lower, compare those three lines first: that is almost always where the difference sits.
